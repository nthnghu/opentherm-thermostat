Boiler control simulator

A deliberately simple house model with radiators and a Victrix-Tera-like boiler (4.3 kW minimum output, flow-setpoint control, cut-out at setpoint + 5 K, anti-cycle timer). The four thermostat strategies run on the same weather, so you can compare comfort, burner starts and gas use. Everything runs locally in your browser.

The model is for learning, not prediction. It has a single-node house (1R1C), one lumped water volume, radiators following Q = Q₅₀·(ΔT/50)^1.3, a boiler that modulates to keep flow at the setpoint and cuts out at setpoint + margin, a t5-style 180 s ramp, and efficiency from return temperature (see condensing.html). The start/stop penalty uses the UCL lab figures (−1.5 % at 180 s burns … −11.8 % at 10 s). There is no solar gain, no DHW, no ignition burst and no hydraulic bypass. Real numbers come from logging the real boiler (chapter 09).

What each strategy does

A · On/off: CH enable when the room is 0.25 K below setpoint, off 0.25 K above. Flow setpoint fixed (default 65 °C). This is how a classic relay thermostat drives a boiler.

B · PI load compensation: every 60 s, flow setpoint = 35 °C + Kp·error + integral, clamped to [boiler t0 = 25 °C, max]. No low-load handling, so it is a "plain PID on ID 1".

C · PI + anti-cycling: the same PI, but when the requested flow is below what the boiler produces at minimum fire, it switches to duty cycling: burns of at least 10 min in periods of at least 20 min (≤3 starts/h). During a burn it holds the setpoint at flow − 3 °C, so the boiler sits at minimum fire instead of hitting its cut-out. This is the Phase 1a recommendation (after SAT's PWM idea).

D · Weather curve + PI trim + anti-cycling: the SAT heating curve from the outdoor temperature, plus a small PI trim (Kp/3, integral ±10 K), plus the same anti-cycling. This is the Phase 1b recommendation, once an outdoor reading is available.
