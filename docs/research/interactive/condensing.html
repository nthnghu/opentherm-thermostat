How to read this

Above about 57 °C return, only sensible heat is recovered and efficiency barely moves: the Victrix Tera 24 Plus datasheet gives 97.8 % NCV at 80/60. Below the dew point, water vapour starts condensing on the heat exchanger and gives up its latent heat. The gain grows the cooler the return gets, reaching about 106–108 % at 50/30 and 40/30. The theoretical ceiling for natural gas is about 111 % NCV, the ratio of gross to net calorific value.

Efficiencies above 100 % appear only because boiler efficiencies are quoted on the net (lower) calorific value, which ignores the latent heat. On a gross basis the same boiler peaks at about 97–98 %.

Return temperature comes from three things: the flow temperature the controller asks for, the ΔT across the radiators (pump speed and radiator size), and anything that lets hot flow bypass the radiators (bypass valves, low-loss headers). That is why the study guide spends so much time on flow-temperature control and on hydraulics.

The curve is an illustrative fit to the Tera's datasheet points (97.8 % at 80/60, about 106 % at 50/30, about 108 % at 40/30) and the 55.6 °C dew point.
